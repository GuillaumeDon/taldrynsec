01 — Overview

Why Cyber Security?

Cyber security careers are becoming more in demand and offer high salaries. The industry spans from offensive pentesting (hacking machines and reporting vulnerabilities) to defensive security (defending against and investigating cyberattacks).

Why Get a Career in Cyber

  • High pay — Jobs in security have high starting salaries.
  • Exciting — Work can include legally hacking systems or defending against cyber attacks.
  • In demand — Over 3.5 million unfilled cyber positions worldwide.
Key Takeaway

There are many different roles in cybersecurity. This room covers 7 of them, split between defensive (Blue Team) and offensive (Red Team) roles. Understanding what each role does helps you choose your path.

Defensive Roles — Blue Team

Roles That Protect and Respond

These roles focus on maintaining security, monitoring for threats, investigating incidents, and analyzing malicious software.

02 — Defensive Role

Security Analyst

Responsible for maintaining the security of an organisation's data.

Security analysts are integral to constructing security measures across organisations to protect the company from attacks. They explore and evaluate company networks to uncover actionable data and recommendations for engineers to develop preventative measures. This role requires working with various stakeholders and understanding the security landscape.

Responsibilities

  • Working with various stakeholders to analyse cyber security throughout the company.
  • Compile ongoing reports about the safety of networks, documenting security issues and measures taken.
  • Develop security plans, incorporating research on new attack tools and trends, and measures needed across teams to maintain data security.

Learning Paths

  • Pre Security
  • Cyber Security 101
  • SOC Level 1
03 — Defensive Role

Security Engineer

Design, monitor and maintain security controls, networks, and systems to help prevent cyberattacks.

Security engineers develop and implement security solutions using threats and vulnerability data — often sourced from the security workforce. They work across a breadth of attacks including web application attacks, network threats, and evolving trends and tactics. The ultimate goal is to retain and adopt security measures to mitigate the risk of attack and data loss.

Responsibilities

  • Testing and screening security measures across software.
  • Monitor networks and reports to update systems and mitigate vulnerabilities.
  • Identify and implement systems needed for optimal security.

Learning Paths

  • SOC Level 1
  • Jr Penetration Tester
  • Offensive Pentesting
04 — Defensive Role

Incident Responder

Identifies and mitigates attacks whilst an attacker's operations are still unfolding.

Incident responders respond productively and efficiently to security breaches. Responsibilities include creating plans, policies, and protocols for organisations to enact during and following incidents. This is often a highly pressurised position with assessments and responses required in real-time, as attacks are unfolding.

Key Metrics

  • MTTD — Mean Time to Detect
  • MTTA — Mean Time to Acknowledge
  • MTTR — Mean Time to Recover

The aim is to achieve a swift and effective response, retain financial standing and avoid negative breach implications. Ultimately, incident responders protect the company's data, reputation, and financial standing from cyber attacks.

Responsibilities

  • Developing and adopting a thorough, actionable incident response plan.
  • Maintaining strong security best practices and supporting incident response measures.
  • Post-incident reporting and preparation for future attacks, considering learnings and adaptations.

Learning Path

  • SOC Level 1
05 — Defensive Role

Digital Forensics Examiner

Responsible for using digital forensics to investigate incidents and crimes.

If you like to play detective, this might be the perfect job. In a law-enforcement department, you would focus on collecting and analysing evidence to help solve crimes: charging the guilty and exonerating the innocent. In a corporate setting, you would use forensic skills to analyse incidents such as policy violations.

Responsibilities

  • Collect digital evidence while observing legal procedures.
  • Analyse digital evidence to find answers related to the case.
  • Document your findings and report on the case.
06 — Defensive Role

Malware Analyst

Analyses all types of malware to learn more about how they work and what they do.

A malware analyst's work involves analysing suspicious programs, discovering what they do and writing reports about their findings. Sometimes called a reverse-engineer, as their core task revolves around converting compiled programs from machine language to readable code, usually in a low-level language. This requires a strong programming background, especially in assembly language and C language. The ultimate goal is to learn about all the activities a malicious program carries out, find out how to detect it and report it.

Responsibilities

  • Carry out static analysis of malicious programs, which entails reverse-engineering.
  • Conduct dynamic analysis of malware samples by observing their activities in a controlled environment.
  • Document and report all the findings.
Offensive Roles — Red Team

Roles That Attack and Test

These roles focus on finding vulnerabilities, testing defenses, and simulating real-world attacks to help organisations improve their security posture.

07 — Offensive Role

Penetration Tester

Responsible for testing technology products for security loopholes.

A penetration tester's job is to test the security of systems and software within a company — this is achieved through attempts to uncover flaws and vulnerabilities through systemised hacking. Penetration testers exploit these vulnerabilities to evaluate the risk in each instance. The company can then take these insights to rectify issues to prevent a real-world cyberattack.

Responsibilities

  • Conduct tests on computer systems, networks, and web-based applications.
  • Perform security assessments, audits, and analyse policies.
  • Evaluate and report on insights, recommending actions for attack prevention.

Learning Paths

  • Jr Penetration Tester
  • Offensive Pentesting
08 — Offensive Role

Red Teamer

Plays the role of an adversary, attacking an organisation and providing feedback from an enemy's perspective.

Red teamers share similarities to penetration testers, with a more targeted job role. Penetration testers look to uncover many vulnerabilities across systems to keep cyber-defence in good standing, whilst red teamers are enacted to test the company's detection and response capabilities. This job role requires imitating cyber criminals' actions, emulating malicious attacks, retaining access, and avoiding detection. Red team assessments can run for up to a month, typically by a team external to the company. They are often best suited to organisations with mature security programs in place.

Responsibilities

  • Emulate the role of a threat actor to uncover exploitable vulnerabilities, maintain access and avoid detection.
  • Assess organisations' security controls, threat intelligence, and incident response procedures.
  • Evaluate and report on insights, with actionable data for companies to avoid real-world instances.

Learning Paths

  • JR Penetration Tester
  • Offensive Pentesting
  • Red Teamer
Summary

All Roles at a Glance

Defensive (Blue Team)

Security Analyst
Explores networks, compiles reports, develops security plans. Entry-level friendly.
Security Engineer
Builds and maintains security solutions. Bridges offensive and defensive knowledge.
Incident Responder
Reacts to breaches in real-time. High-pressure role focused on MTTD/MTTA/MTTR.
Digital Forensics Examiner
Investigates incidents by collecting and analyzing digital evidence. The "detective" of cyber.
Malware Analyst
Reverse-engineers malicious programs. Needs assembly and C knowledge.

Offensive (Red Team)

Penetration Tester
Tests systems for vulnerabilities through systemised hacking. Finds as many flaws as possible.
Red Teamer
Simulates real attacks over weeks. Tests detection and response, not just vulnerabilities. Avoids detection.
Pentester vs Red Teamer — Key Difference

Pentester = find as many vulnerabilities as possible across systems. Red Teamer = simulate a real adversary, test detection & response capabilities, maintain access, avoid getting caught. Red team assessments are longer (up to a month) and more targeted.

Vocabulary

Key Terms

  • Security Analyst — Evaluates networks and develops security plans.
  • Security Engineer — Designs and implements security solutions.
  • Incident Responder — Handles security breaches in real-time.
  • Digital Forensics Examiner — Investigates incidents using digital evidence.
  • Malware Analyst / Reverse Engineer — Analyzes malicious programs through static and dynamic analysis.
  • Penetration Tester — Tests systems for vulnerabilities through ethical hacking.
  • Red Teamer — Simulates real attacks to test detection and response.
  • MTTD — Mean Time to Detect (how fast you spot an attack).
  • MTTA — Mean Time to Acknowledge (how fast you respond).
  • MTTR — Mean Time to Recover (how fast you recover).
  • Static Analysis — Examining code without running it (reverse-engineering).
  • Dynamic Analysis — Running malware in a controlled environment to observe behavior.
